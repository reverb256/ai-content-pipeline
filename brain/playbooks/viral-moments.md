# Viral Moments Playbook

> How we detect the moments worth clipping, decide which to clip, and wire
> every clip to revenue. This is the "find the outlier, clip it, convert it"
> layer — the difference between publishing content and building an engine.

## The Core Method: Outlier Detection → Decode → Clip → Convert

Most content performs near the channel average. A few pieces outperform
3x-10x. Those outliers reveal what the audience actually wants. We do not
guess what will go viral — we study what already did, then clip our own
content for those moments.

```
competitor outlier scan → decode the moment → clip our flagship → distribute
       ↑                                                      ↓
  our own performance outliers ←────────────────── track clip conversion
```

## 1. Detect Outliers (competitors + own)

### Competitor Outlier Scan (weekly, scout)

For each competitor channel/account in the niche:

- Find videos/posts performing 3x+ above their channel average
- Record: topic, title pattern, hook, thumbnail style, length, posting time
- Ask: what topic + format + packaging made THIS one break through?
- Store candidates in `campaigns/<name>/outliers/` (link + what to steal)

### Own Outlier Detection (weekly, editor/analyst)

- Track our own posts: impressions, replies, bookmarks, saves, conversion
- Flag anything 2x+ above our average
- Record: hook type, angle, format, platform, CTA, offer
- Every outlier is a HYPOTHESIS to test again — not a universal rule

## 2. Decode the Moment

Every outlier has identifiable parts. Break it down:

| Component | Ask |
|-----------|-----|
| Hook | What stopped the scroll in the first 2-3 seconds? |
| Tension | What conflict or surprise held attention? |
| Pattern interrupt | What broke the expected rhythm? |
| Specificity | What concrete number/detail made it credible? |
| Payoff | What did the reader get / feel at the end? |
| CTA | What action did it prompt, and did it match the stage? |

A moment is clip-worthy when it has: a standalone hook, a complete micro-
narrative (beginning-middle-end in under 60 seconds), and a payoff that works
without context.

## 3. Clip It (distributor)

Every flagship piece produces 5-10 clip candidates. Each clip:

- **Is standalone** — works for someone who has never seen the flagship
- **Has one idea** — one hook, one insight, one payoff
- **Is platform-native** — vertical for Shorts/Reels/TikTok, text for X,
  carousel for LinkedIn
- **Has a revenue-appropriate CTA** — matched to the reader's stage

Clip candidate list per flagship:

```
1. The hook moment (the first 30 seconds, re-cut as a cold open)
2. The surprising claim (the counterintuitive insight, isolated)
3. The proof point (the number/receipt that makes it credible)
4. The mechanism (the "how it works" explainer, standalone)
5. The failure story (what broke — vulnerability + lesson)
6. The payoff (the end state / result, as a standalone)
7. The roadmap (the reusable framework, compressed)
8. The CTA moment (the offer / conversion point, front-loaded)
```

## 4. Convert (revenue wiring)

Every clip and every flagship has a revenue answer. Not a hard sell — a
matched CTA:

| Reader stage | Revenue action |
|--------------|---------------|
| Aware (first exposure) | Follow / bookmark / subscribe (build the asset) |
| Interested (engaged with the idea) | Read the flagship / join the list |
| Considering (wants the implementation) | The offer: template, guide, product |
| Ready (trusts the system) | The product / consulting / founding tier |

**Revenue rules:**
1. Every campaign names its offer up front (`campaign.md` → Offers section).
2. The CTA must match the reader's stage — a "buy now" on an aware-stage clip
   is revenue-negative.
3. Track conversion per clip, not just engagement. A clip that gets 100k views
   and 0 conversions is a distribution win and a revenue miss.
4. Affiliates/tools we recommend are tagged and tracked (the tools ARE the
   offer for our audience).

## 5. Feed the Loop

- Clip performance updates this playbook (what clip format converts)
- Outlier findings update hooks.md + angles.md
- Revenue data updates offers.md
- Every week: keep / test / stop (see performance.md)

## Anti-Patterns

- Clipping the whole video into 10 identical versions (no standalone value)
- Posting the same CTA on every clip regardless of stage
- Chasing viral moments that don't fit the audience or the offer
- Measuring clips by views only (bookmarks/saves/conversion matter more)
- Stealing a competitor's exact hook without our own angle

## Metrics That Matter

| Metric | What it tells us |
|--------|------------------|
| Clip → flagship CTR | Does the clip make people want the full piece? |
| Bookmark/save rate per clip | Deep value vs. surface entertainment |
| Conversion per clip | Revenue wiring working? |
| Outlier repeat rate | Did we turn the win into a repeatable format? |
| Time-to-conversion | How fast does a viewer move through the stages? |
