## Verified Claims (NTSB & Investigation Process)

6. **NTSB investigates all civil aviation accidents in the US and significant
   events in other modes.** Since 1967, >154,000 aviation accidents investigated;
   >15,700 safety recommendations issued to >2,470 recipients; 82% of closed
   recommendations implemented. In 2025 alone: 1,436 investigative reports
   adopted (+18% over 2024), 131 new safety recommendations (14 urgent). Source:
   https://www.ntsb.gov/about/reports/Documents/2025%20ARC%20Final%20Published.pdf

7. **The NTSB Party System leverages industry expertise** by designating
   organizations/companies as parties to investigations (manufacturers, FAA,
   airlines, unions). Parties provide technical expertise during fact-finding;
   legal/litigation persons are barred. Parties do NOT participate in
   analysis/report writing but may submit proposed findings. FAA is automatically
   a party by law. Source:
   https://www.ntsb.gov/investigations/process/Pages/partysystem.aspx ;
   https://www.ntsb.gov/investigations/process/Documents/NTSB%20Party%20Guidance.pdf

8. **The "Go Team" is the core NTSB response unit** — on-call 24/7, dispatched
   to accident scenes. Specialists cover operations, structures, systems, ATC,
   weather, human performance, survival factors, powerplants, metallurgy, CVR/FDR.
   Led by an Investigator-in-Charge (IIC). Source:
   https://www.ntsb.gov/investigations/process/Pages/goteam.aspx

9. **NTSB investigation timeline: typically 12-24 months** from launch to
   probable cause determination. Process is not strictly linear (phases overlap).
   Classification system ranges from Class 1 (major, transport-category) through
   Class 4 (limited scope, 6-month final report). Source:
   https://www.ntsb.gov/investigations/process/Pages/default.aspx ;
   https://ntsb.gov/about/organization/AS/Pages/aviation-classification.aspx

10. **NTSB Aviation Investigation Search database is public** — contains civil
    accidents and selected incidents from 1962 to present. Queryable by date,
    location, FAR part, injury level. Source:
    https://www.ntsb.gov/Pages/AviationQueryv2.aspx

## Contradictions

- **Opportunity card claims $12-25 RPM for aviation/tech niche.** Verified data
  shows Transport RPM is $5.69 (median) with P25-P75 range $3.17-$9.81. The
  $12-25 figure is likely a CPM (advertiser-paid) estimate or top-quartile RPM,
  not median. Use the AIR Media-Tech verified median ($5.69) for conservative
  planning; $12-25 is achievable only at the top end of Education-straddling
  content with strong ad load and US audience.

- **"No dominant aviation education brand" vs established channels existing.**
  Mentour Pilot, Pilot Institute, and Airplane Academy are real brands with
  100K-400K subscribers. The gap is not "no content" — it's that no channel
  owns the specific "sourced + diagram-heavy + non-sensational explainer"
  framing systematically. Nuance matters: the gap is editorial positioning,
  not market absence.

## Unknowns / What Sources Do NOT Prove

- **No verified RPM data specific to aviation-only YouTube channels.** AIR's
  Transport niche includes cars, trains, planes, builds — only 7 channels in
  the sample. Aviation-specific RPM could be higher (educational framing) or
  lower (smaller audience). Use the verified Transport median with appropriate
  uncertainty bounds.

- **No public data on "diagram-heavy aviation explainer" audience size** is
  available pre-production. The demand signals (tweet engagement) are real but
  do not translate directly to view-count estimates.

- **The 1,200 aviation accidents/year figure from NTSB does NOT mean 1,200
  video opportunities/year.** Most GA accidents have recurring causes and do
  not produce new safety information (per NTSB's own classification system).
  Fresh, distinct safety lessons are far rarer than the headline number implies.

- **Regional airline starting salary data ($90K-$103K) is 2026-specific and
  fluctuating.** The pilot shortage that drives these numbers may ease if
  training output increases or demand shifts. Quote as "as of 2026" with source
  date.

- **Whether AI-generated incident animations are acceptable to the audience is
  untested.** NTSB reports include diagrams and animations (their own), but
  audience tolerance for AI-generated aircraft footage vs real stock footage
  in this niche is unknown. Prefer real footage/animations to AI-generated
  aircraft imagery.

## Mechanisms Worth Explaining

1. **The Party System** — how the NTSB leverages manufacturer/airline expertise
   while maintaining independence. Why parties can't be in legal roles, why they
   don't write the report, and why this produces better factual records than a
   purely in-house investigation. This is unique to transportation safety.

2. **Why aviation is safe (selection bias explanation)** — @Ecaspu's point:
   media covers crashes but not the 99.999% of flights that go right. The
   NTSB's actual safety recommendation pipeline (131 new recommendations/year,
   82% adoption rate on closed recommendations) is the invisible story.

3. **The ATP 1,500-hour rule as economic structure** — why hour-building via
   CFI instruction ($30K-$70K earned while building hours) beats paying
   $150K-$240K to rent aircraft. This is the economic logic that makes an
   airline career accessible, and it's counterintuitive to outsiders.

4. **Part 61 vs Part 141 as a cost/speed tradeoff** — structured academies
   reduce total hours (190 vs 250 for CPL) and unlock R-ATP (1,000-1,250 hrs)
   but cost more. The 60-hour difference at $150-$180/hr = $9,000-$10,800
   savings, plus earlier airline entry.

5. **How an NTSB investigation actually proceeds** — Go Team dispatch, working
   groups, factual reports, public docket, Board meeting. Timeline: 12-24
   months. Alaska Airlines 1282 door plug: investigation launched Jan 2024,
   final report adopted June 2025 (17 months). DCA mid-air collision Jan 2025,
   urgent safety recommendations issued March 2025.

6. **GA vs commercial safety gap** — general aviation has dramatically higher
   fatal accident rates than Part 121 airline operations. GA: 0.68 fatal
   accidents per 100,000 hrs (2024). Commercial scheduled: orders of magnitude
   lower. Why the gap exists and what it means for "aviation safety" claims.
