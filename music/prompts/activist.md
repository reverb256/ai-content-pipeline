## Lyrics guidance

The lyrics ARE the genre here. Write them human, direct, chantable — the chorus is
what a crowd sings back at a rally, probably without your recording.

```text
[Verse 1]
(solo guitar + voice: concrete imagery, one specific injustice,
plain words, no abstractions)

[Pre-Chorus]
(band enters, tension builds, short lines)

[Chorus]
(GROUP VOCALS in unison: the title line repeated, 4-8 words max,
chantable at a march — "get up, stand up" is the bar)

[Verse 2]
(the personal stakes — verses are YOUR platform; quotable lines
that could be repurposed as an inspirational quote)

[Chorus]
(group vocals, same words, bigger)

[Bridge]
(strip to voice and stomps: call-and-response,
lead line answered by the group)

[Chorus]
(final: clap layer doubles, everyone sings)

[Outro]
(title line once, quiet, solo guitar rings out)
```

Rules:
- **Title = the main chorus lyric.** People must be able to find the song from what
  they remember singing. "Stop! What's That Sound" over "For What It's Worth".
- Chorus: simple, direct, first person plural where possible. Verses: your platform,
  concrete images ("broken bottles on the children's feet"), not slogans.
- Say the change you want (Kendrick's "Alright" paints the desired future); pure
  rage dates badly and narrows sync use.
- The song must work sung unaccompanied by a crowd. Test it: can you chant the chorus
  walking?

## More Options

- **Weirdness:** 30%. Sincerity is the currency. Novelty reads as satire in this
  genre and kills documentary sync.
- **Style Influence:** 70%. Folk-gospel structure holds; the topical content must
  come from YOUR lyrics, not Suno's.
- **Exclude Styles:** ` autotune, trap beats, EDM drops, aggressive metal, party anthems, club`
- One exception: for hip-hop-format activist tracks, swap the style palette
  (boom-bap drums, spoken-word flow) but keep every lyric rule above.

## Craft notes — what makes it read as authentic activist music

1. **Chorus = the crowd's part.** That is why it is called a chorus. Group unison
   vocals, repeated title, 4–8 words. "Get up, stand up: stand up for your rights" is
   the gold standard of chantable.
2. **Call-and-response is the genre's oldest technology** — from work songs and
   spirituals through gospel to "Say it loud — I'm black and I'm proud." A protest song
   that never hands the mic back is a speech with chords.
3. **Verse imagery beats slogans.** "Southern trees bear a strange fruit" outlived a
   thousand chants because it made the listener see. Verses carry the reportage;
   the chorus carries the demand.
4. **Sincerity over polish.** The vocal must sound like a person telling the truth,
   not a product. Minimal processing; the wobble is the witness.
5. **Quiet-loud dynamics**: solo-guitar verse into group chorus mirrors the
   one-voice-to-many-movement arc that is the genre's dramatic engine.
6. **Copyright strategy:** human-authored lyrics make the track registrable (US
   Copyright Office: human authorship required) and acceptable to the distributors
   that reject 100%-AI content. Register disclosing the AI portions; claim the words.
7. **Ad-suitability:** political content limits monetization. Do NOT build this
   lane on ad revenue — direct-fan and documentary sync are the pro outlets.

A generation with party energy, club production, or a chorus nobody could chant
while marching is OUT of genre — reject it.
