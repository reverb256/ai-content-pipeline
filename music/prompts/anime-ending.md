## Lyrics guidance

Vocals are Japanese — write lyrics in **Japanese script** for correct
pronunciation. The ending is the emotional exhale after the opening's
explosion; its density is lyrical, not rhythmic.

```text
[Intro]
(Japanese: 2 lines, sparse. Piano alone, one
string sustain. The hook melody arrives in
fragment — endings plant the chorus seed early)

[Verse 1]
(Japanese: 4-8 lines. Intimate, first-person.
The memory or the person the episode left behind.
Plain words, close-mic vocal feel, piano + bass only)

[Pre-Chorus]
(Japanese: 2-4 lines. Strings enter, drums brush in,
the line lengthens — the first swell)

[Chorus]
(Japanese: 4-8 lines. The emotional core, singable,
royal-road harmony. Full strings + piano + bass together.
Title phrase repeated once)

[Verse 2]
(Japanese: 4-8 lines. The cost named honestly —
what was gained, what was left. Same meter as verse 1,
guitar arpeggios join the piano)

[Pre-Chorus]
(Japanese: repeat, fuller, drums settle to a groove)

[Chorus]
(Japanese: repeat, bigger — brass-free, strings carry)

[Bridge]
(Japanese: 2-4 lines. Strip to piano + voice again.
The quietest point of the song. This is where the
listener remembers the episode's last scene)

[Final Chorus — KEY LIFT]
(Japanese: same words, gentle lift up a key — the
ending's version of the opening's key change, but
warm, not explosive. Strings swell an octave,
vocals reach up, then settle)

[Outro]
(Japanese: 1-2 lines. Final line hangs on a single
held piano note, long reverb tail into silence.
No hard cut — the ending fades the light, not the door)
```

Rules:
- **Final chorus lifts a key, gently.** The key change is the
  genre's gesture even in ballad territory — but the ending's
  version is a warm swell, not the opening's explosion. Same
  move, different gain.
- Japanese script only. Kana/kanji, not romaji.
- Endings are dense with MEANING, not energy: no wasted bars,
  but phrases are allowed to breathe. Space between notes is the
  ballad's equivalent of the opening's double-kick.
- IV–V–iii–vi under the chorus, same as the opening — the royal
  road's bittersweet landing is why endings and openings share it.

## More Options

- **Weirdness:** 30%. Ballad form is load-bearing (sparse intro,
  pre-chorus swell, bridge breath, gentle key lift). Chaos breaks
  the emotional contract.
- **Style Influence:** 80%. "92 BPM + royal road + Japanese ballad
  vocals + key-lift final chorus" must survive intact.
- **Exclude Styles:** ` EDM, heavy drums, distorted guitars, rap, autotune, English lyrics`
- Do not exclude "orchestral" or "piano" — they ARE this idiom.

## Craft notes — what makes it read as an anime ending (not a generic ballad)

Cite-check against real anison convention before changing this template:

1. **Tempo 70–120 BPM; ballad pocket 85–100.** Below 70 is a
   film-score cue, above 120 is mid-tempo pop. The ending lives
   where the brush drums can breathe between the piano's 8ths.
2. **The royal road (王道進行, IV–V–iii–vi) carries the bittersweet
   landing.** Open on the subdominant, resolve to minor vi — the
   same progression as the opening, but the ending uses its minor
   color instead of its triumph. This shared harmonic DNA is why
   an opening and an ending of one series sound like one world.
3. **The key lift is the genre gesture, at ending gain.** Final
   chorus up a key (semitone), strings swelling an octave — the
   same signature move as the opening, but warm and settling.
   Omit it and the track is a generic ballad.
4. **Piano + strings + clean guitar, brushes not sticks.** The
   ending's percussion is textural: brushed snare and rim clicks,
   entering no earlier than verse 2. A full drum kit at bar one
   is an opening wearing an ending's title.
5. **Density of meaning, not energy.** Every line must carry the
   episode's emotional residue. The bridge strips to piano + voice
   because the quietest moment is where the listener remembers.
6. **Japanese vocals in Japanese script.** Same rule as the
   opening — kana/kanji for pronunciation, human-authored lyrics
   for copyrightability (see the lyrics gate).
7. **The tail is part of the song.** Endings end on a held note
   with a long reverb tail into silence — the fade of the TV
   credit light. A hard cut or a pop fade is an opening's ending.

A generation with a hard unison ending, a fast drum intro, or
English lyrics is OUT of genre — reject it.
