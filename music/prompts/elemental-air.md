Rules:
- Everything rises or suspends. Air never lands: avoid resolved cadences; let
  phrases end open (sus2, add9, no leading-tone pulls).
- Space IS the instrument. More silence than notes.
- Loop-safe ending: the last flute tone disperses into the wind bed of bar 1.

## More Options

- **Weirdness:** 45%. Air tolerates shimmer and strangeness in the textures; keep
  the flute phrases singable.
- **Style Influence:** 70%. "Flute + chimes + sus2 + weightless" must hold.
- **Exclude Styles:** ` bass drops, heavy drums, EDM, dark minor, tense thriller, epic brass`
- The "epic brass" exclusion is load-bearing — brass turns sky into war.

## Craft notes — what makes it read as an AIR theme

1. **Csus2 is air's signature chord.** The suspended second — no third, no
   resolution pressure — is the "unresolved, spacious, floating" sound the
   elemental-chime tradition assigns to Air. The tuning lacks the fourth and
   seventh: nothing in it demands arrival. Build phrases that way.
2. **Flutes are the element's voice** — breath made audible. Breath attack, air
   through the tone, short trade-phrases between two registers (alto + pan) reads
   as wind currents in dialogue.
3. **Wind chimes and glass mallets** provide the sparkle layer: unpredictable,
   high, unquantized. Their randomness IS wind.
4. **No weight.** No bass below the pads, no drum kit. Rhythm, if any, is a
   shimmer or a breath pulse. Gravity = wrong element.
5. **Upward-moving figures.** The melodic grammar of heights is ascent and
   suspension; endings disperse (fade upward into chimes) rather than resolve
   (land on the tonic).
6. **92 BPM with rubato feel.** Fast enough to feel wind moving, slow enough to
   never rush — a drifting current, not a march.
7. **Loop discipline:** stems + loop_point + seamless_loop, outro into intro
   wind bed.

A generation with heavy low end, drum groove, or resolved cadences is OUT of
genre — reject it.
