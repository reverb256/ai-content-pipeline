## Lyrics guidance

Choir only — no solo lyrical singing. Latin or pseudo-Latin chant syllables as
TEXTURE (the "Fortis, Astra, Dominus" register). Mark it explicitly so Suno does not
invent English lyrics:

```text
[Intro]
(sub-drone, distant metallic echoes, whispered choir breaths,
heartbeat pulse)

[Choir]
(low male chant murmurs, one held chord, no drums)

[Build]
(percussive clock ticks, reverse risers, taiko enters,
staccato string ostinato starts the engine)

[Instrumental]
(brass states the fanfare theme over full percussion)

[Choir]
(chant doubles the theme, medium intensity, syllabic)

[Breakdown]
(near-silence: one sub-boom, a breath, a single string
sustain — the false ending)

[Chorus]
(the climax: full orchestra + full choir "AHH" hits,
brass fanfare at maximum, every layer, sub impacts on
the downbeats)

[Outro]
(sudden breathless cut: one final deep boom, short reverb
tail, silence)
```

Rules:
- Choir = "AHH"/"OHH" syllables and Latin-esque chant. Write the actual syllables in
  the lyrics box if the chant needs specific words (e.g. "for-tis, as-tra");
  otherwise use [Choir] + (chant) direction.
- Three-act trailer form is the product: dread → escalation → false-ending silence →
  wall of sound. The silence before the climax is load-bearing.
- Sync lane: sudden stop-starts and hard hits are the cut points editors need.
  Do not smooth them away.

## More Options

- **Weirdness:** 40%. Scale and aggression welcome; the three-act form must survive.
- **Style Influence:** 80%. "Ostinato + taiko + choir + braams" must hold or it is
  generic "epic."
- **Exclude Styles:** ` pop vocals, EDM drop, trap, lo-fi, ambient drone, cheerful major`
- Do not exclude "EDM" wholly — hybrid trailer music keeps synths and risers.

## Craft notes — what makes it read as a BOSS theme (not generic epic)

1. **Ostinato strings = the nervous system.** Fast repeated low-string figures are
   the engine of every modern trailer/boss cue; they cut through the percussion
   weight and give editors rhythmic sync points. No ostinato = wallpaper.
2. **Taiko + timpani battery, not a drum kit.** Boss-music percussion is drums-as-
   artillery; taiko carries ritual-scale, timpani carries classical threat.
3. **Choir is a weapon, not a pad.** Syncopated choral HITS and chant that DOUBLES
   the brass theme turn "big music" into "world-ending." Generic sustained vocal
   pads are the #1 tell of AI epic slop — this template forces hits and chant.
4. **Braams** (huge distorted low-brass swells) + fifth-heavy power chords carry
   threat and classic game-VGM grammar at once.
5. **Three-act form is the trailer contract:** brooding low-end intro → layered
   escalation → breakdown silence (the "one-liner" slot) → maximum-impact re-entry.
   Skip the silence and the climax lands as a nudge, not a punch.
6. **Sudden breathless finale.** End on a hit and short tail — the logo card. Fade
   outs are a defect in this genre.
7. **C minor, 150 BPM.** Dark-key convention; 140–160 is the boss pocket. Slower
  reads as trailer-tease, faster as action-shred.
8. **This is the one genre where "generic epic" is the failure mode to engineer
  AGAINST.** The discriminators: chant that doubles the theme (5), syncopated choir
  hits (3), a false ending (5), and an ostinato that never stops driving (1).

A generation that is a sustained pad soup, fades out, or peaks without the
false-ending silence is OUT of genre — reject it.
