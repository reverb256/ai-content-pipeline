# sentimental-piano — prompt template

> Registry: family `acoustic`, lanes: sync, streaming, direct-license, youtube-longform.
> Sync-friendly by design: slow, predictable structure, easy to clear, mood-discovery
> trend. Felt piano is the anchor sound of the genre.

## Style field (paste into Suno "Styles", 674/1000 chars)

```text
neoclassical sentimental piano, 72 BPM, intimate felt piano close-mic'd with soft hammers and audible pedal and key mechanism noise, one singable 4-6 note motif repeated with tiny variations, simple diatonic harmony with add9 sus2 and sixth color tones, chord changes every 2-4 bars, broken-chord left hand ostinato, gentle rubato pacing, small chamber cello and upper strings entering only at the emotional peak, one soft reversed swell before phrases, dark spacious reverb around the piano but the piano itself stays close and dry, mid-register focus, low velocities, no virtuosic runs, one emotional bloom then a gentle return to solo piano, nostalgic and hopeful at once
```

## Lyrics guidance

This genre is instrumental. Leave the **lyrics box empty** — that is Suno's
instrumental path. If a wordless vocal
texture is wanted (an "ahh" swell at the peak), it is a texture, not a lyric.
The tag sheet below is OPTIONAL structure control: section tags and parenthetical
direction, never singable words.

```text
[Intro]
(solo felt piano, the motif, alone, pedal noise audible)

[Verse]
(the 4-chord loop, right hand states the motif)

[Verse]
(same loop, one note changes, wider voicing)

[Bridge]
(cello enters underneath, upper strings sustain one color note)

[Chorus]
(the emotional bloom: slightly fuller harmony, one higher melodic
note, string swell — NOT a dramatic explosion)

[Verse]
(dissolve — strings fall away, piano alone again)

[Outro]
(the motif, softer than the intro, let the reverb tail end it)
```

Rules:
- One motif, one bloom. Over-composing (many ideas, dense counterpoint) is the
  #1 failure mode of this genre.
- Rubato: the model should feel a human performer, not a quantized grid.
- The piano is the foreground. Strings/atmosphere are the air around it, never
  competition.

## More Options

- **Weirdness:** 30%. This lane sells predictability to sync supervisors. Weirdness
  breaks the "easy to clear, easy to cut" property.
- **Style Influence:** 75%. "Felt piano + motif + slow diatonic" must hold; leave room
  for fresh melodic turns.
- **Exclude Styles:** ` drums, percussion, EDM, synths, pop vocals, dramatic trailer crescendo, virtuosity`
- The "dramatic trailer crescendo" exclusion is load-bearing: the bloom is a
  whisper, not a wall of sound.

## Craft notes — what makes it read as sentimental piano

1. **Felt piano is the genre's anchor sound.** Soft hammers, mechanical noise (pedal,
   keys) audible, close-mic'd, slightly dark. A bright concert-grand tone reads as
   classical recital, not sentimental sync music.
2. **Motif: 2–6 notes, singable, stepwise.** State, repeat with one small change,
   resolve. Einaudi's own benchmark ("songwriting, not composing" — one breath, one
   wave) is the target; ~81 BPM is his pocket, 72 keeps it more still.
3. **Harmony: diatonic + color.** add2/add9/sus2/6, changes every 2–4 bars, common
   tones preserved. No strong functional tension — modal ambiguity IS the warmth.
4. **Left hand = the engine.** Broken-chord or 1–5–8 ostinato, strict tempo while the
   right hand rubatos against it. The strictness underneath makes the rubato feel
   human.
5. **Strings: small, secondary.** Solo cello or chamber trio, sul tasto sustains, one
   slow swell into the peak. Large string sections = film score, wrong genre.
6. **Mix: close piano, distant world.** Piano dry and present; strings/textures dark,
   reverb-washed, blurred. One reversed swell before a phrase is the signature
   "breathing" transition.
7. **Sync-usable structure:** slow harmonic rhythm and predictable 2–4 bar phrasing
   are what make the track easy to cut to picture. Protect them.

A generation with drums, synth pads as the lead voice, or a virtuosic run is OUT of
genre — reject it.
