## Lyrics guidance

Optional vocal trance. If used, the vocal lives in the breakdown and the second drop.

```text
[Intro]
(no vocal — kick, hats, bass, one filtered pluck)

[Verse]
(quiet vocal phrase, 2-4 lines, airy and sparse)

[Build]
(riser, snare roll, gated pad under the vocal)

[Breakdown]
(NO KICK — the main melody reveals on piano, vocal enters over it,
let the reverb tail breathe a full bar)

[Build]
(riser, snare roll 8ths to 32nds, kick filters back as a ghost)

[Chorus]
(the drop: kick, offbeat bass, supersaw lead carries the anthem,
vocal ad-libs on the downbeats)

[Bridge]
(second breakdown — reharmonise the same melody over new chords)

[Chorus]
(final drop — one new element: octave-up lead double)

[Outro]
(no vocal — strip to kick, hats, bass for the DJ mix-out)
```

Rules:
- The breakdown is the emotional centre; the drop is the payoff. Never let the drop
  arrive unearned.
- Vocal lines: short, open vowels, words that can float. The anthem is carried by the
  supersaw, not the singer.
- Section lengths in multiples of 8 bars (usually 32). Change something small every 8,
  bigger every 16.

## More Options

- **Weirdness:** 35%. Trance form is load-bearing — 32-bar sections, the two-beat gap
  before the drop, the DJ intro/outro. Chaos dissolves the form.
- **Style Influence:** 75%. "138 BPM + supersaw + offbeat bass" must survive intact.
- **Exclude Styles:** ` trap, dubstep, half-time, big room, festival EDM drops, hip-hop`
- Do not exclude "EDM" broadly — trance IS EDM; that exclusion strips the pads.

## Craft notes — what makes it read as uplifting trance

1. **138 BPM is the pocket.** 136–142 is the range; 138 is where a 4/4 kick and a
   rolling 16th offbeat bass sit without crowding. Below 128 or above 145 is a
   different genre wearing trance's clothes.
2. **The offbeat bass is the engine.** Mono, sidechained 4:1 to the kick, plays the
   three 16ths after each kick and skips the kick's own 16th. The ear should read it
   as rhythmic pressure, not a melodic line.
3. **Supersaw = stacked detuned saws** (JP-8000 heritage, detune 40–60/128, two leads
   an octave apart). One saw is not an anthem. Cut their low shelf below ~250 Hz so
   they stop clouding the bass.
4. **Harmony:** i–VI–III–VII (Am–F–C–G) carries the genre; voice pads on sus2/add9
   with one common note ringing through every change.
5. **Form is the genre.** Intro (drums only, 32–64 bars for the mix-in), breakdown
   64–96 bars with its own three-act arc, drop capped at 32–64 bars (fatigue is the
   enemy of emotion), the two-beat silence before the drop — that gap is the single
   most important half-bar in the track.
6. **Dotted-8th delay** (≈326 ms at 138) and hall reverb on the lead; plate on the clap.
   Classic-era trance has almost no sub-bass below 40 Hz — do not ask for it.

A generation with a half-time drop, a trap hat, or no DJ-usable intro is OUT of
genre — reject it.
