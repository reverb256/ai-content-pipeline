# visual: anime-ending (anime-ending)

> Pairs with: `prompts/anime-ending.md`. Video shape: album-30-60min.
> The ending's visual is the last shot of an episode: a quiet room,
> the light going, someone looking back.

## Palette & scene

- Dusk palette: warm amber #ffb347 fading to deep blue-violet #2b2d5c,
  a single practical light source (window / streetlamp / train window).
- Scene: an interior or a train window at dusk — the view outside is
  a soft bokeh of lights. A figure sits by the window, seen from
  behind or in silhouette, small against the glass.
- The horizon of the song is the city light dissolving into night.

## Motion grammar

- Camera drifts slowly (0.3–0.5 px/frame) — the ending never rushes.
- Verse = static or near-static, dust motes drifting in the light beam.
- Pre-chorus = the light outside dims one stop; camera begins a slow
  tilt up.
- Chorus = the bokeh lights bloom gently, slow parallax, no strobe —
  warmth spreads, never flashes.
- Bridge = near-still: one mote crossing the frame, the light held.
- **Final chorus key lift = the last light brightens one stop and the
  camera rises slightly** — a warm exhale, the visual twin of the
  gentle key lift. Then it settles.
- Outro = the light fades slowly to a single point, then a slow fade
  to black over the reverb tail. The fade is correct here — the ending
  genre fades the light, the opening genre cuts the door.

## Texture

- Soft grain 5%, bloom 8% on the practical light only. Shallow depth
  of field: the figure and the light sharp, the rest dissolving.
- Typography: none during the song; end card with the album/title in
  the last 2 seconds, chapter-safe.

## Loop & variation (gate_youtube_authentic)

- Each song gets its own window, light color, and city bokeh pattern.
  The dusk-to-night gradient deepens as the album progresses — track
  9 is the last light in the record.

## Audio-visual sync

- Piano = the dust motes' drift rate. String swell = bokeh bloom.
  Brushed drums = a soft ripple on the window glass. The bridge's
  piano+voice = the held frame. The key lift = the one-stop brightening.
  The final held note = the light point shrinking; the reverb tail =
  the fade to black.
